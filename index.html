Short Film · 2020-2022

AKA 赤

Dorothée Murail ・ Paris / Tokyo

Image

It began with an image, before any words: the monochrome of a body painted red, inside a red box.

AKA follows a butoh dancer through seven psychological states, a descent into the depths of the soul that leads to rebirth. Butoh, the Japanese dance form born in postwar Japan, asks the performer to inhabit emotion entirely, through the body and the face. Here the descent is built into the film itself: with each state, a layer of the set falls away and the space contracts, while the dancer takes on a new weight of feeling. Three cameras film at once: the whole body, a fragment of skin, and a view from directly above, where the figure becomes a marionette of her own mind.

The film doesn't try to explain the seven states. It rests on a simpler belief: some things we live through cannot be said in words, but the body can show them.

Analogue Photography · 2021

Surfaces

Sankai Juku ・ Tokyo

Image

→ click to view the series, 8 images

The starting point of Surfaces is the Japanese concept of honne and tatemae: words for the inner self and the face we show the world. This double life runs through all of Japanese society, and I wanted to give it a body.

Butoh was the obvious language for that: a dance where nothing is acted, where what lies beneath is brought up to the skin. Surfaces was made in Tokyo with Dai Matsuoka and Akihito Ichihara, dancers of the Sankai Juku company. I had seen them on stage in Paris before moving to Japan; the memory stayed with me, and working with them had been a dream ever since.

The images move through that gap: white bodies without faces; then a face appears; then water, a way of crossing to the other side. Some are shot on redscale, the film loaded back to front so the image is taken through its reverse side. Two faces of the same material, one shown and one hidden. The film does what we all do.

Analogue Photography · since 2018

In Between

Analogue photography

Image

→ click to view the series, 16 images

An old woman waits out the cold rain under an umbrella, wearing sunglasses. A horse stands in a garden like an apparition. In Between is made of moments like these, where the world quietly contradicts itself and refuses to settle into a single reading.

No one is ever entirely one thing. The gap is physical: between two bodies, two countries, water and earth. It is mental too: loving nature while living in concrete and iron, believing one thing while inhabiting a world built on another. The images work in pairs, two frames holding one tension in the space between them, or a single frame strong enough to carry the contradiction alone.

The photographs were gathered over years of travel, and nearly all of them on islands. Nothing in them is named or located, but the island is the series' quiet condition: a piece of land held by water, neither sea nor mainland, an in-between you can stand on. The figures pass through it, faces unseen, half here and half elsewhere.

Curation · 2025

Shape of Light

Lin Syouki ・ KURA Gallery, Tokyo-Shibuya ・ May 2025

I wanted to show Lin's work the moment I saw it. The paintings are beautiful and playful at the same time: the reflective surface changes as you move, so you have to move, your body and the light, to discover a different image from every position held in a single piece. Stand still and the painting closes. Step sideways and it opens again.

Curating the show meant building for that. Not hanging objects on walls, but shaping a space where the work could do what it does: ask the viewer to move, to be present, to complete the picture. Six years in Japan taught me to treat emptiness as material rather than absence, and that is the principle the exhibition was built on.

Shape of Light was Lin Syouki's first solo exhibition.

Dorothée
Murail

Dorothée Murail is an image-maker working between Paris and Tokyo. Her work explores thresholds: between cultures, between states, between what shows itself and what resists being seen.

Trained at La Cambre and Les Gobelins, she has built over ten years a practice crossing photography, film and visual research, shaped by six years of living and working in Japan. Her personal projects have been presented at Centre Pompidou and Nuit Blanche Kyoto; her short film AKA received a jury prize at Signs of the Night. Beneath the finished work runs a constant practice of looking, the research that precedes and feeds every project. In 2025 it took the form of Shape of Light, the first solo exhibition of painter Lin Syouki, which she curated in Tokyo.

Alongside her personal work, she photographs and directs for houses and institutions including Cartier, Dior and Vogue.
