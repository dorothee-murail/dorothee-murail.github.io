Short Film · 2020

AKA 赤

Dorothée Murail ・ Paris / Tokyo

Image

It began with an image, before any words: a body painted red, inside a red box. No horizon, no exit.

AKA follows a butoh dancer through seven states of psychological descent, from the loss of identity to inner collapse. Butoh, the Japanese dance form developed in the 1970s, asks the performer to inhabit emotion entirely, through the body and the face. Here the descent is built into the film itself: with each state, a layer of the set falls away and the space contracts, while the dancer takes on another weight of feeling, more exposed with every step. Three cameras film at once: the whole body, a fragment of skin, and a view from directly above, where the figure becomes a marionette of its own mind.

The film doesn't try to explain the states it stages. It works from a simpler conviction: that some conditions of being resist language, and that the body, under enough pressure, can make them visible.

Film Photography · 2021

Red Scale

Sankai Juku ・ Tokyo

Image

Red Scale was made in a Tokyo studio, face to face with Dai Matsuoka and Akihito Hichihara, dancers of the Sankai Juku company. I had seen the company on stage in Paris before moving to Japan, and the encounter stayed with me; working with them was not a casting choice but the starting point of the project.

The series explores honne and tatemae, the gap between the inner self and the social face, a concept I met on arriving in Japan and that became a way to read everything around me. Butoh holds exactly that tension. It does not perform a character; it surfaces what lies beneath one. No model or stranger could have carried this, only a body trained to bring the interior to the skin.

The images are shot on reversed film. Loaded backwards, the emulsion turns everything toward red and rust, so that each picture arrives already altered, showing one thing while another burns underneath.

Film Photography · 2020

In Between

Film photography

Image

In Between is built in pairs. Two images placed side by side, holding a single emotion between them, or one image strong enough to carry that suspension alone. The work lives in the gap, the space the eye crosses from one frame to the other.

Nothing in these images is named or located. They could be anywhere, and that is the point. What recurs is a quality of in-between: thresholds, waiting, the moment before or after something, a place caught between one state and the next.

Human figures appear, faces unseen. They don't play roles or anchor a story. They pass through the image without owning it, anonymous, present for a moment, then gone. They are humanity itself, caught in passing, moving through a world that holds still for no one.

Curation · Tokyo · 2025

Shape of Light
Lin Syouki

KURA, Shibuya ・ May 2025

I saw Lin's work before I understood it, which is exactly what made me want to show it. The pieces don't resolve from a distance. The reflective surface shifts as you move, so the image you see is one you partly make yourself. Stand still and it closes. Step sideways and it opens again.

Curating the show meant building for that. Not hanging objects on walls, but shaping a space where the work could do what it does: ask the viewer to move, to be present, to complete the picture. Six years in Japan taught me to treat emptiness as material rather than absence, and that is the principle the exhibition was built on.

Shape of Light was Lin Syouki's first solo exhibition. Eighteen works, shown at KURA in Shibuya, May 2025.

Dorothée
Murail

An image-maker whose work explores thresholds — between cultures, between states, between what shows itself and what resists being seen.

Trained at La Cambre and Les Gobelins, she has developed over ten years a practice that crosses photography, cinema and visual research. Her personal projects — rooted in cultural tensions and interstitial spaces — have been presented at Centre Pompidou and Nuit Blanche Kyoto.

In parallel, she collaborates with leading houses and institutions — Cartier, Dior, Vogue — on projects where the image is at stake. Her curatorial work, born from a visual archive built over several years, extends the same posture: not to classify the world in images, but to reveal its frictions.
